=== Readout from Friends of CAWG meeting

Last Friday we held a four-hour hybrid working meeting on the future of CAWG, with about 30 participants from across industries.
The goal was to give the co-chairs clear direction on scope, organizational structure, governance, trust partnerships, and naming.

Headline: strong convergence on scope and structure, one clear approval (the new home), and a dated plan to be operational by mid-December.

==== Scope: CAWG is the identity layer

CAWG is the home for the creator and identity layer of the Content Credentials ecosystem. The focus is making clear who asserted what, about which asset, and in what capacity, for individuals as well as organizations.
CAWG stays tied to C2PA, and our assertions live in a C2PA manifest. That dependency wasn't up for debate (with a footnote).
Stable creation facts (who, when) stay in the manifest.
Rights, licensing, consent, and training/data-mining permissions change by time, territory, ownership, and contract; C2PA doesn't provide a mechanism for mutable data so we will instead prefer to define standardized, unambiguous links to authoritative external sources.

Interoperability is a priority: DDEX, WIPO Global ID, and other identity and metadata systems.

Industries populate their own profiles. CAWG defines the structure, extension points, and recognition requirements.

==== New home: independent, unfunded JDF project

Approved without objection: CAWG becomes an independent, initially unfunded Joint Development Foundation project under the Linux Foundation.

* IP carries forward on the W3C Patent Policy. JDF also requires antitrust and conduct agreements.
* CAWG stays separate from C2PA but pursues a formal liaison relationship.
* The steering committee can later move to a funded model if needs arise.

==== Trust governance

Two layers: CAWG sets the top-level framework and baseline requirements, and external organizations run trust lists, conformance, and industry-specific validation.
CAWG decides which frameworks qualify for recognition. It won't operate registries or conformance programs.
We separated who you are (identity) from what you are (roles like news organization or musician). Self-regulating industry bodies, such as IPTC, would attest to the latter.

Concern raised: inconsistent validators could fragment the ecosystem. Common validation requirements are a charter priority, but there's no complete solution yet.
The current identity assertion relies on S/MIME governance for content created through 31 March 2027. That date is provisional, and we need to extend or replace the arrangement before it becomes urgent.
Details such as baseline requirements, designation criteria, and designation terms are still to be drafted.

==== Steering committee

Direction: small, capped, odd-numbered, around 9 to 11 seats.

Set participation expectations up front, with term limits, rotation, and removal for prolonged non-participation.

Representation goals: industry, organization size, geography, for-profit and nonprofit, individual creators, labor and creative organizations, broadcasters, advertising, and civil society, with a cap on technology companies.
Advisory or tiered participation will give broader groups a voice without bloating the committee.

News and broadcast need a smaller follow-up (Bruce, Mike, and others) on how representatives are chosen and held accountable.

==== Naming

The organization, the technical standard, and any consumer trust mark may carry separate names.

A small naming task group — to be formed — brings back two or three cleared options in 2-3 weeks; Scott and I finalize the name shortly thereafter.

Criteria: international usability, clarity, IP clearance, and a credible B2B standards name.

We'll consult C2PA stakeholders before choosing anything that echoes their name, since some C2PA participants have concerns about identity work being associated with C2PA.
No name and no consumer mark has been chosen.

==== Timeline and what's next

* *Today, Mon 5 Oct:* We issue the call for patent exclusions. This starts a waiting period that we're planning around as 30 days, ending 4 Nov. _Risk:_ if it turns out to be 45 days (ending 19 Nov), formation, the working group start, and the announcement could slip by up to two weeks.
* *This week:* We ask the full DIF Steering Committee to review CAWG's exit as a DIF working group, with approval targeted for late October. That approval is a dependency. Technical work continues in DIF until the new organization can accept contributions.
* *Fri 16 Oct:* Draft project and working group charters go to Linux Foundation JDF staff.
* *Fri 30 Oct:* Circulate a unified CAWG 2.0 draft, giving about two weeks of review.
* *Late Oct to mid-Nov:* LF legal and steering committee review the charter. Then the JDF project is formed, IP agreements are re-signed, and specs and repos migrate.
* *Mid to late Nov:* The new technical working group convenes to review and approve CAWG 2.0.
* *Late Nov:* Public announcement, avoiding Thanksgiving week.
* *Late Nov to Dec:* We draft baseline requirements and designation criteria, and begin trust partnership designation conversations with X.509 candidates and GLEIF.
* *Mid-December:* CAWG is operational as an independent JDF project with its own steering committee. This is aggressive and depends on the name, charter, legal transition, and DIF approval.
* *Q1 2027:* First designation agreement(s) with trust governance partners.
