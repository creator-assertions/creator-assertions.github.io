=== Readout from trust task force: Scott

🎥 _8'30":_ Scott reported on three work streams. The task force continues work with Trust Over IP on Trust Registry Query Protocol transactions, but has paused that effort while prioritizing a CAWG certificate policy that can support a CA trust list.

Scott will bring proposed policy updates to the next task-force meeting.

The third work stream is a governance-framework template for industry-specific use of CAWG assertions. The task force plans to build on Trust Over IP's governance metamodel, which is being moved into GitHub, after the certificate policy is further along. The Trust Task Force will meet the following Monday despite the U.S. Thanksgiving week.

=== Consent assertion

🎥 _25'40":_ Scott asked about the status of the consent assertion. Erik said it is ready for review and a vote, and shared the https://cawg.io/consent/1.0-draft+initial-version/[consent specification] and https://github.com/decentralized-identity/cawg-consent-assertion/pull/5[consent assertion PR #5]. Eric proposed giving members two weeks to review it and scheduling a vote at the next meeting. Scott and Richard agreed to be reviewers; other members who rely on consent were invited to comment.

The group discussed whether consent belongs in CAWG if the organization focuses on immutable claims. Erik argued that consent is inherently changeable, and that a signed claim can point to an authoritative registry for current terms and a history of changes. Scott suggested that CAWG could make an immutable claim that consent was asserted at a particular time, potentially with a timestamp, while industry-specific systems maintain current consent. Richard noted the practical difficulty of updating assets already distributed when consent changes. No decision was made on scope or ratification; the draft will return for a vote after review.

Andy suggested involving https://www.linkedin.com/in/ryanschnizlein/[Ryan Schnizlein] for expertise in AICP broadcast-TV-spot contractual-delivery requirements.

*ACTION:* Scott, Richard, and Jeremy to review consent PR #5; members to provide feedback before the vote at the next meeting.

=== Naming the new organization

🎥 _52'14":_ Eric described name-screening criteria: avoid “Foundation” because the project will be hosted by the Linux Foundation; avoid terms that collide with existing organizations or C2PA terminology; and retain a visible but not overly close relationship to C2PA. When Philippe questioned excluding “provenance,” Eric agreed to revise that criterion.

The group discussed risks in “identity” and “trust”: identity could obscure the group's work on metadata and consent, while “trust” might imply that a consumer should trust the truth of an organization's claims. Members emphasized that CAWG adds human and organizational claims, including attribution and credit, to a cryptographically bound C2PA record; it does not describe the record of digital-object changes that C2PA calls provenance.

🎥 _1h03'46":_ C3A emerged as the leading draft name, expanded in chat as “Coalition for Creator Claims and Attribution.” Members supported the direction but continued to discuss alternatives and wording, including C3MA (“Coalition for Creator Claims, Metadata and Attribution”), C-Trust, CAITE, TRUENESS, and C4A. “Credit” was suggested as a more familiar alternative to “attribution.” The group noted that C3A domains are largely taken, while c3ma.com and c3.ma were suggested as available options.

No final organization name was adopted. Eric closed by proposing that the group continue with C3A or a variant and look for a name that is meaningful and does not have problematic collisions. The legal organization name can be separate from a consumer-facing brand, which may need marketing input. Nathan shared the https://proofmode.org/cawg_user_guide[Proofmode CAWG user guide] as an example of current user-facing communication.

*ACTION:* Eric to continue name-collision research and bring a C3A or variant forward for broader feedback.
